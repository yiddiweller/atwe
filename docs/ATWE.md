## 1. Atwe in one paragraph

A customer messages you. You send a quote from inside the conversation. They accept it, it becomes an invoice with a pay button, and they pay it by card from that button. When someone buys from your shop with protection, the money is held until they confirm it arrived, and the review afterwards moves your reputation. Nothing had to leave Atwe, and nothing had to be typed twice.

That is Atwe: one app for the whole of a working life. You run a business, work for yourself, create, or work for someone else, and you also have friends, family and a feed. Atwe keeps all of it under one name. In one account a person has a profile and a feed, private and group messaging with calls, a shop, a wallet with a real balance, quotes and invoices, a jobs board, a calendar, a business dashboard, and an assistant, Atwe AI, that knows the whole product and can act on their behalf once they confirm. Because all of it is one account, one thing leads to the next.

**The approved descriptions.** These four are the only wording to use when Atwe has to be described at a fixed length. Cut nothing else down; pick the one that fits.

- **The line (seven words):** Atwe. Where you and your business live.
- **A sentence (for a social bio or a store subtitle):** Atwe is one account for a person and a business alike: your feed, your messages and calls, your shop, your wallet, your jobs and your reputation, connected.

- **The full paragraph (for a press boilerplate):** the two paragraphs at the top of this section, used together and unchanged.

## 3. The slogan

The official Atwe slogan is:

**Atwe. Where you and your business live.**

It is the founder's own idea with nothing added and nothing to trim. "You and your business" names the two lives one Atwe account holds. "Live" says it is a home, not a tool you visit. The name in front makes it a slogan rather than a strap: read alone on a poster, a bus side or an app store page, it still says who is speaking. It is literally true today, on the web app people already use, and it will still be true after the 2027 release and the 2028 launch. It does not say "online", because a home does not need to explain where it is, and a word like that dates a line within a few years. The "one place" half of the idea is carried by the word "where": there is one place, and this is it.

**Where it came from.** The founder's direction, verbatim: "ATWE. A ALL IN ONE PLATFORM. WHERE YOU AND YOUR BUSINESS LIVES ONLINE. (It's like your second home...)". Three ideas live in that sentence: one place, you and your business living online, and a second home. The slogan keeps all three. "All in one platform" is what every large app says about itself and cannot be owned, so it may appear in body copy and never as the line itself. "A second home" is the feeling behind the line: the place you keep coming back to, where your things and your people already are. It is the campaign's emotional territory, and a campaign idea can be built on it, but it is not the slogan.

**How to write it.**

- Always exactly: Atwe. Where you and your business live.
- Two sentences, two full stops. The full stop after Atwe is part of the line; never replace it with a colon, a dash or a line break alone.
- Capitalise Atwe and the first letter of Where. Every other word is lower case. Never set it in all capitals except where a typeface system demands it for every heading on the page.
- The short form, for a space where the name is already visible right beside it (an app store subtitle, a social bio, the cover of this document): Where you and your business live. If the name is not on the page, use the full line.
- Never add an exclamation mark, a question mark, a trademark symbol or an emoji.
- Never append "online", "in one place" or any other qualifier to the line. If the idea needs more words, put them in a sub-line beneath it, not inside it.
- Never translate it loosely. In another language, keep the shape: the name, a full stop, then "where you and your business live".

**Lines for specific jobs.** Three runners-up, each kept for a place the slogan itself does not fit.

- *One home for you and your business.* A sub-line under a photograph, where the name is already on the page and the warmth of "home" carries the frame.
- *Your business lives here. So do you.* A two-beat line for film and audio, where the turn in the second sentence can land as a spoken beat; also right for a business-owner audience being shown the personal side.
- *Make yourself at home. Bring your business.* A welcome line for a new member, and a natural opening for a printed piece or a launch invitation.

## 4. The idea

**Positioning, in one paragraph.** For people who run something, and for everyone they deal with, Atwe is the one account where a person and their business live online. Unlike a stack of separate apps, the messages, the money, the shop, the jobs and the reputation are one connected thing, so nothing has to leave and nothing is typed twice.

Most people who work for a living use a dozen separate products to do one job: a messaging app to talk to customers, a payment app to get paid, a job board to hire, a shop platform to sell, a social network to be seen, a booking tool, an ad manager, an invoicing tool, a video call service. Each holds one slice of the same relationship, none of them talks to the others, and the person in the middle carries everything between them by hand.

Atwe's founding idea is that all of those slices belong together, because they are all about the same people. A customer is also a contact, also a follower, also a payer, also a reviewer. A supplier is also a message thread, also an invoice, also a connection. When the pieces live in one place, the product can do things a collection of separate apps never can:

- A conversation can carry a product, a quote, an invoice, a payment or a split bill as a live card inside the chat, not as a link to somewhere else.
- A payment can be held safely until both sides are satisfied, because the platform can see both the money and the order.
- An assistant can answer "who messaged me about the blue chairs, and did they pay?" because the messages, the listing and the wallet are all in reach.
- A reputation can be worked out from real completed dealings, not from a self-reported star count.
- A business and a creator can find each other, agree a deal and settle it without leaving Atwe.

Inside the company this advantage has a name: context. Context is the reason all of the above is true. It is never the word said in public; the public words are the slogan and the three pillars in Section 6.

## 5. Who Atwe is for

**The primary audience is the person who runs something small.** A trade, a shop, a salon, a restaurant, a studio, a practice, a freelancer. One owner, no IT department, and a working day that today runs across a stack of subscriptions that do not know about each other. Three moments in that person's own words, and Atwe's answer to each:

- *"A customer asked for a price on one app, paid me on another, and reviewed me nowhere."* On Atwe the quote is sent inside the conversation, accepting it makes the invoice, the pay button is in the same thread, and a completed dealing is what a review is tied to.
- *"I sent the invoice as a PDF and chased it by text for three weeks."* On Atwe the invoice is a card in the chat with a pay button on it, a due date, and a one-tap reminder you can send while it is unpaid.
- *"My shop, my till and my social account do not know about each other."* On Atwe they are one shelf: the storefront, the in-person till and a product tagged on a post all sell from the same stock, and the takings land in the same numbers.

**The secondary audiences** are the creator turning an audience into a business (subscriptions, tiers, pay-per-view, brand deals, a studio), and the two sides of hiring: people looking for work and the businesses hiring them, on a real two-sided jobs marketplace rather than a listings page.

**The network is everyone those people deal with.** Their customers, followers, friends and family, who arrive because somebody they deal with is already here, and who use Atwe to buy, book, message, pay and keep up without ever running anything. They are how Atwe grows, and they are welcome to use it as a plain social and messaging app.

A person and a business are the same kind of thing on Atwe: an account with a name and an @username. A business is not a separate page that someone manages; it is an account in its own right, which is what lets it message, sell, hire, post and be reviewed like any other member. The founder should confirm the primary audience above before the campaign is briefed; the product supports all three, and a campaign has to lead with one.

## 6. What to say, and what never to say

**The promise** is the slogan: Atwe. Where you and your business live.

**Three pillars carry it.** Everything else in this document is support for one of these three.

1. **One account, a person and a business alike.** No page to manage, one name, one feed, one shop, one wallet, one reputation. The proof: a business is an account; a quote sent in a chat becomes the invoice and the payment; a product tagged on a post, in a Daily or on a Short is bought from the same stock as the shop and the till.
2. **The money is safe.** A protected order holds the payment until the buyer confirms it arrived, and releases itself so a seller is never held hostage. Money is never invented: there is no coin, no token and no fake currency, a refund comes out of what was actually paid, and the refund paths are tested against one rule, that afterwards every balance, every held payment and Atwe's own take still add up to what was paid in. The only credits with no payer behind them are the rewards Atwe itself funds, a referral bonus or redeemed points, and each is a real ledger entry. Daily and weekly limits stand in the way of fraud.
3. **It works for you.** Atwe AI knows the whole product, reads your own account, answers "who messaged me about the blue chairs" from what it finds, and does anything a finger can do without a password, but only after showing you a card and waiting for you to confirm. It is not allowed to act on a stranger's words.

**Claims a headline can carry.** Each is one sentence, with the feature it rests on and the caveat that keeps it true. Write "on Atwe", not "only Atwe", unless the claim is about the combination or about a rule in Section 9; single pieces exist elsewhere, and what is Atwe's alone is that they share one customer, one wallet, one conversation and one reputation.

- Never say "ID-verified members" or "everyone here is verified". The identity label is granted by Atwe staff after a check; there is no automated document check.

## 9. How Atwe behaves, and how it speaks

Atwe has rules it follows, and each is enforced in the software rather than written in a policy. They are the product's character, and the campaign can lean on every one of them.

**Money is never invented.** Every movement of a member's money is a transfer from somewhere to somewhere; the only exceptions are rewards Atwe itself pays for. Refunds come out of what was actually paid. Points and gift cards are real balances with real ledgers. There is no in-app coin, no token, no fake currency of any kind. A community boost buys a higher place in Discover and nothing else; a gift sent during a live is real money in the host's wallet.

**Both sides are protected.** A protected order holds the payment until the buyer confirms and releases it automatically so a seller is never held hostage. A courier is never told they have the job before the fee is secured, and gets the fee only when the buyer confirms it arrived, types the handoff code at the door, or lets three days pass without a word. A cancelled event refunds everyone. Where the sales advance is switched on, a quiet month repays it slowly.

**Trust is earned from real events.** A reputation is computed from completed dealings, never typed in, and the workings are visible. Certification is not for sale: it is earned at 90 or more with ten completed dealings, staff can grant or withhold it, and every such decision is logged.

**The assistant proposes; the person disposes.** Atwe AI never performs an action without a confirmation card, refuses anything that needs a password, and is not allowed to act on words a stranger wrote.

**Honesty is stated in the product.** The notice in every chat says plainly that standard messages are not end-to-end encrypted, and the security page says exactly what is. A highlight on a listing is derived from a fact Atwe holds, never written for effect. A count that could not be taken shows as a blank, not a zero. "Sent" is only ever said when something was sent. A feature that is built but not yet allowed says so instead of pretending.

**It refuses the shortcuts.** Some things Atwe will not do, even when they would be easy and profitable: no chart music behind someone's video without a licence; no third-party bot that reads a whole group; no captions stored without telling anyone; no message to a customer who asked you to stop; and Pro is cancelled from the plans page in one tap, and both the plan and the recurring charge stop at once.

**It is designed with restraint.** A black background by default, with a light theme that keeps every other rule. One white primary action per screen. Blue for identity, not decoration. Green for money and success, red for destructive, yellow for information. Full pills, no outlines, no emoji in the product's own words. Plain, calm copy. The Atwe mark is a swirl, and it is the only mark the product uses for itself, including for Atwe AI, where the rest of the industry uses a sparkle.

### How Atwe speaks

Campaign copy follows the product's own rules without exception, because a member will see both in the same day.

**Six rules.**

1. Short sentences. If a sentence needs a second breath, it is two sentences.
2. Say the true thing strongly, not the big thing vaguely. "Send the quote from inside the conversation" beats "transform how you do business".
3. Volunteer the limitation before the reader finds it. It is why the product is believed.
4. The product is the subject, not the adjective. Atwe holds the payment; Atwe is not "a seamless payments experience".
5. No exclamation marks. Ever.
6. No questions the reader cannot answer. "Ready to unlock your potential?" is not a sentence a person can reply to.

**Banned words.** Revolutionary, game-changing, seamless, cutting-edge, unleash, empower, supercharge, next-generation, world-class, ecosystem, super-app, all-in-one (in a headline; it may sit in body copy), frictionless, disruptive, powerful (as a bare adjective).

**Three rewrites.**

- "Unleash your business potential with Atwe's all-in-one platform" becomes "Your till, your shop and your feed sell from one shelf."
- "Seamless payments you can trust" becomes "Buy with protection and the money waits until you say it arrived."
- "Next-generation AI that works for you" becomes "Ask Atwe AI who messaged you about the blue chairs. It looks, and it tells you."

**The mechanics, which are not negotiable.** No long dashes in anything Atwe writes; use a full stop, a comma, a colon or brackets. No emoji. No sparkle beside Atwe AI; the mark is the swirl. "Atwe AI" is always two words. The five worlds are Home, Beam, Engine, Notifications and Account, exactly. The named things are Dailies, Circles, Collections, Shorts, the till, Atwe Pro, Atwe Certified and Atwe Card, exactly.

**Lines already in Atwe's voice.** These may be used as written and must not be improved.

- Nothing had to leave Atwe, and nothing had to be typed twice.
- Money is never invented.
- The assistant proposes; the person disposes.
- A gift, never a stake.
- Both the buyer and the seller approve the courier, or nobody moves.
- Tap the number and see what moved your reputation.
- A business is not a page you manage. It is an account.
- Atwe tells you which chats are not end-to-end encrypted.

## 10. Everything Atwe does

This section is deliberately complete. Atwe is a large product, and an agency planning its story needs to know its full extent before choosing what to say; the campaign should choose a few of these and say them strongly rather than repeat the list. An agency reading this for the first time will find the breadth hard to believe, which is a fair reaction. Every line in this section is built, is live on the web today, and can be opened at the address in the appendix, with the exceptions the text names where they appear: the Atwe Card is a waiting list, and the sales advance is switched on market by market. A few parts (shipping labels, carrier rates and sales tax, the business phone number, voice-note transcripts, live market prices and picture generation) are built into the product and run through an outside provider that Atwe switches on per deployment; without one, the feature says so and steps aside. Two more parts are built and switched on by Atwe rather than always on: item authentication and the creator revenue share.

### 10.1 Being seen: the profile and the feed

*One page that answers the question every customer, employer and stranger has: can I trust this person, and what have they actually done?*

**The profile.** A banner, a photo, a headline, a bio, a location, a website, a join date, and industries. Tabs for Posts, Replies, About, Media and Likes, with a Business tab for business accounts. A pinned post. Plain facts about a person (what they do, where they live, languages, how long they have been doing this) in a curated list that stays scannable. Work history, education, licences and certifications, skills with peer endorsements and quiz-verified skill badges, written recommendations with the subject's approval, a featured row and a portfolio ("Showcase") with likes and comments. A profile strength meter that nudges the next missing piece. A personal QR code, and a scanner. Three trust numbers together at the top: reviews, rating, and years on Atwe. A public Q&A on every business profile, with the business's own answer marked and shown first. A business picks the one primary button on its profile: Book, Order or Message.

**Verification, layered honestly.** A verified badge, a neutral silver seal, that a member applies for and Atwe approves; it marks an account Atwe has looked at, not a checked identity. Atwe Pro members carry a Pro badge beside their name. Business verification in levels, from documents to a premium seal, with a review step. An affiliation badge showing the organisation a member belongs to, granted by the business or approved by staff. An identity-verified label, separate from the badge, that Atwe staff grant after a check and that shows when and how it was checked; there is no automated document check today, and the label says out loud that a check has safeguards but cannot guarantee that somebody is who they say. Workplace verification through a business email domain. An Open to Work ring on the photo, with a visibility control.

**The feed.** For You is ranked on engagement and recency and then personalised to the reader: their industries, the hashtags they follow, the authors they engage with and linger on (recency-weighted, so old habits fade), their recent searches, and their "Not interested" signals. A few fresh, low-engagement posts are woven in so new people get a chance. Same-author and same-topic posts are spread out. Following is purely chronological. The feed is endless and does not recycle what you saw in the last three hours unless that is all there is left to show. A new member is asked what brought them here, which topics to follow and which people, with starter packs per industry, so the feed reflects them from the first minute rather than after a week of guessing.

**Posts.** Text, up to four photos in a mosaic, video (long and landscape included), GIFs, polls, documents (a PDF, a deck, a price list), bold and italic, alt text written by hand or by Atwe AI, location, tagged people, up to five co-authors, and up to five of your own products tagged on the media so a reader can buy from the post. Threads of up to 25 connected segments. Articles: formatted long-form pieces published under your name, for the thinking that does not fit a post. Celebration posts for milestones: opened a store, a hire, an anniversary. Quote posts and reposts with attribution, and a quick quote straight from a saved post. An hour-long edit window with an "Edited" label. A ten-second undo after posting. Drafts saved to your account, and scheduling to a future time.

Still to finish: groups, media, reactions and calls in Beam; buying inside the app; storefront management and business analytics; Apple Pay; home-screen widgets; and the public App Store listing, which waits on Apple approving the company's own developer account (the upgrade from the founder's individual account to an organisation one, which needs the company to exist first). Android builds from the same code and needs a Play developer account, not more code.

**Behind it.** Photos, video and documents can live on a content delivery network, so a video can be up to two gigabytes and three hours long. Every fault a member hits is reported to the operator as the screen, the error and the build, with no page address, no message text and no names in it, and every server error carries a short reference the member can quote.

**How it is checked.** Standing checks run on every change: sixty screens are opened in both themes and any text below the readable floor fails; every control is measured against the 44-point touch minimum on a phone and a tablet; a real sale, a real call and a shared photo are driven end to end between two accounts; every request carries a deadline so a lost signal cannot freeze a button; scrolling is measured under a throttled processor; and no long dash can reach a member from anything Atwe wrote.
